ASTRONOMICAL SOLAR TRACKER

The purpose of this project is to compare its functionality against the classical simple solar project that uses LDRs to arbitrarily track the sun. The method in this project is dual axis astrological tracking of the sun, that uses the panels location, time, and date to calculate the suns position and interpret that position into servo motor angles to face a solar panel towards it.

Project introduction

At boot the ESP32 joins Wi-Fi and sets its clock from an internet time server. Every 30 seconds it calculates the sun’s compass direction (azimuth) and height (elevation) for its location, turns those into angles for an east–west servo and a tilt servo, and moves the panel. At night it parks the panel flat.

Major design choices

  • Calculated sun position. Working out the sun’s position from the clock keeps tracking steady under cloud coverage. The cost is that the clock, location and mount alignment all have to be accurate, resulting in higher ammounts of computational requirement.
  • Internet time. The ESP32 already has Wi-Fi, so time comes from an NTP server with no extra hardware.
  • One servo per angle. An azimuth-elevation mount turns each calculated angle straight into one servo position, keeping control simple.
  • Update every 30 seconds. The sun moves about 0.125° in that time, less than a servo’s 1° step, so moving more often would only waste power.
  • Park below 5° elevation. Near sunrise and sunset the panel collects little energy, so it lies flat instead of chasing a low sun.

Design

The sun’s position comes from Meeus’ solar equations (Astronomical Algorithms), the same method behind NOAA’s solar calculator. From the time \(T\) in Julian centuries, they give the sun’s declination \(\delta\) and right ascension \(\alpha\). Then, for latitude \(\varphi\) and longitude \(L\):

Hour angle
\[ H = \text{GMST} + L - \alpha \]
Elevation
\[ \sin h = \sin\varphi\,\sin\delta + \cos\varphi\,\cos\delta\,\cos H \]
Azimuth
\[ \cos A = \frac{\sin\delta - \sin\varphi\,\sin h}{\cos\varphi\,\cos h}, \qquad A \to 360^\circ - A \ \text{ if } \sin H > 0 \]
Refraction
\[ R = \frac{1.02}{\tan\!\left(h + \frac{10.3}{h + 5.11}\right)} \ \text{arcmin} \]
Servo angles
\[ \theta_{EW} = 180\,\frac{A - 90}{270 - 90}, \qquad \theta_{NS} = 180\,\frac{h}{90}, \qquad \text{both clamped to } 0\text{–}180^\circ \]

  • Hardware: east–west servo on GPIO 18, tilt servo on GPIO 19, both driven with 500–2,400 µs pulses. Every limit and mapping is a setting at the top of the sketch.
  • Logging: each update prints a CSV line (time, azimuth, elevation, both servo angles, status) over serial for checking and plotting.
  • Self-recovery: Wi-Fi is checked every 60 s and reconnected, with a fresh time sync, if it drops.

Results

Simulations of this project on Wokwi prove promising, however real world testing is required to confirm efficiency of the design.

Future developments

  • GPS for time and location. Removes the need for Wi-Fi and manual coordinates. Useful for a portable tracker, not necessary for a fixed installation.

This is the first and one of the key functional components of a small personal solar station, which is what this project is building towards. Future developments will include farme design, MPPT functionality, and a small BESS system.
